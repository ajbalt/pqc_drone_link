# SP 800-90A/B/C (Random Bit Generation)

**Summary**: Joint source summary of NIST's three random-bit-generation documents,
which are always used together: 800-90A (DRBG mechanisms), 800-90B (entropy sources)
and 800-90C (RBG constructions).

**Sources**: [sp-800-90a](sources.md#sp-800-90a), [sp-800-90b](sources.md#sp-800-90b), [sp-800-90c](sources.md#sp-800-90c)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-08

---

## What they are

| Document | Covers | Version |
|----------|--------|---------|
| SP 800-90A | DRBG algorithms: Hash_DRBG, HMAC_DRBG, CTR_DRBG; strengths; reseeding | Rev. 1, June 2015 |
| SP 800-90B | Entropy sources: noise sources, conditioning, health tests, validation | January 2018 |
| SP 800-90C | How to build an approved RBG from the two: RBG1/2/3, RBGC (DRBG trees, including OS RBGs) | Final, September 2025 |

Together they define the "approved RBG" that FIPS 203, FIPS 204, SP 800-227 and
SP 800-232 require.

## Takeaway

None of the three mentions Linux or `getrandom()`. SP 800-90C §7 allows for OS RBGs in
principle, but every component must be validated. That answers
[Q7](open-questions.md#q7-how-do-we-state-the-getrandom-deviation) as a documented
deviation.

## Extraction notes

All three extracted with docling. The undecoded formulas (32, 75 and 13 respectively)
are in algorithm and statistical-test details we don't use.
